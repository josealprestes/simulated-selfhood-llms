## 2. Philosophy of Science: Systematic Analysis

Following Bunge (1973, ch. 1), the philosophical approach adopted in this study is **systematic analysis** — the most rigorous mode of engagement with real science, distinct from apriorism, preface analysis, textbook analysis, historico-philosophical analysis, and isolated item analysis.

Systematic analysis bears on whole units of scientific research (theories, complete experimental cycles) and examines each item in its proper context, reducing the risk of misinterpretation. This approach is operationalized in the study by:

- Treating LLMs not as isolated objects of philosophical speculation, but as **specific theoretical models** embedded in a broader computational and linguistic research framework.
- Grounding every interpretive claim in behavioral evidence derived from a structured experimental protocol (generation, replication, statistical aggregation, and human-evaluation validation).
- Rejecting purely aprioristic frameworks (e.g., strong functionalism or strong eliminativism applied *a priori*) in favor of hypotheses that can be confronted with empirical data.

The guiding methodological slogan, following Bunge (1973, p. 10), is: *Primum cognoscere, deinde philosophari* — first know, then philosophize.

## 3. Generic Theory vs. Specific Theoretical Model

Bunge (1973, ch. 5) draws a crucial distinction between:

| Category | Definition | Testability |
|---|---|---|
| **Generic theory** | Extremely general factual theory with no reference to a particular kind of stuff (e.g., information theory, automata theory) | Not directly empirically testable per se |
| **Specific theoretical model** | Theory of a particular system or class of systems; specific theory within a generic framework | Directly testable — confirmable and refutable |

This distinction directly informs the study design:

- The claim that LLMs exhibit **pseudo-consciousness** or **simulated selfhood** belongs, at the level of formulation, to a generic theoretical framework (philosophy of mind + computational linguistics). It is not directly testable *per se* in its most abstract form.
- The study operationalizes this generic claim into a **specific theoretical model**: the hypothesis that, if LLMs produce self-referential outputs with genuine introspective coherence (analogous to self-awareness), then repeated self-focused prompts should elicit stable, consistent, and semantically coherent regularities across independent runs and temperatures. This operationalization is directly testable.
- The empirical finding — that the observed patterns are fragile, regime-sensitive generative effects rather than stable introspective regularities — constitutes evidence *against* the specific model, and by extension challenges the generic attribution of introspective coherence to LLMs.

## 4. Testability: Beyond Verificationism and Falsificationism

Bunge (1973, ch. 2) argues that neither verificationism (Vienna Circle) nor strict falsificationism (Popper) provides an adequate criterion for scientific status. A theory can be:

- **Empirically untestable per se** yet **scientifically legitimate** if it is precise, formally consistent, and fruitful for deriving testable specific models.
- **Confirmable** without being simply a summary of observations.

This position is adopted here with respect to theories of mind and consciousness: the hypothesis of pseudo-consciousness is treated as a **scientifically legitimate generic conjecture** that (a) has a precise formulation derivable from the philosophy of mind (Searle, Dennett, Baars, Bender et al.), and (b) generates specific, testable predictions about LLM behavioral outputs. The study tests those specific predictions.

Testability criterion applied: **confirmability of derived specific models** + **coherence with the broader body of scientific knowledge** (Bunge 1973, p. 29–43).

## 5. Scientific Metaphysics: Realism, Materialism, and Levels

Bunge (1973, chs. 8–10) defends a **scientific metaphysics** — a body of general theories about the world, conceived rigorously and in agreement with contemporary science. The core commitments relevant to this study are:

### 5.1 Scientific Realism
The outputs of LLMs are real behavioral events in a physical substrate. Their analysis does not require commitment to any form of idealism or operationalist reduction. The study treats outputs as *evidence about the generative mechanism*, not as exhaustive definitions of it.

### 5.2 Dynamic Materialism
Following Bunge's materialism (1973, p. 169–185), mind is treated as a functional property of a physical system. Consciousness — including its necessary conditions of **intentionality**, **rationality**, and **subjectivity** — is grounded in the organization of biological neural systems. This is not a trivially reductive position: it acknowledges emergent properties (ch. 9, *Levels hypothesis*) while denying any non-physical substrate.

Consequences for the study:
- LLMs do not instantiate consciousness because they lack the biological material substrate that grounds intentionality and subjectivity, however refined their linguistic outputs may be.
- The apparent coherence of LLM self-referential outputs is a **surface-level generative phenomenon**, not evidence of underlying self-awareness. This is consistent with the *stochastic parrot* characterization (Bender et al., 2021) and with Searle's distinction between intrinsic and derived intentionality.

### 5.3 Hypothesis of Levels of Organization
Bunge (1973, ch. 9) argues that reality is organized in levels (physical, chemical, biological, mental, social), each with emergent properties irreducible to lower levels alone. Language and intentional meaning belong to the mental/social level and emerge from biological organization. Statistical pattern matching — which is the operational substrate of LLMs — belongs to a lower level of description and does not, by itself, generate the properties characteristic of higher levels. This provides a principled ontological argument against attributing genuine intentionality to LLMs.

## 6. The Axiomatic Structure of the Behavioral Model

Following Bunge's treatment of axiomatics in physics (1973, ch. 4), the behavioral model of this study can be made explicit in terms of three classes of postulates:

**Formal Assumptions (FA)**
- FA1: LLM outputs are strings produced by a stochastic function over token sequences.
- FA2: Temperature parameterizes the entropy of the output distribution.
- FA3: Consistency metrics (semantic similarity, logical coherence) are well-defined over output pairs.

**Physical/Empirical Assumptions (PA)**
- PA1: If a system has introspective self-awareness, then its self-referential outputs should exhibit stable regularities independent of sampling stochasticity.
- PA2: The behavioral regularities observed in LLM outputs are products of training data distribution, not of self-knowledge.

**Semantic Assumptions (SA)**
- SA1: "Introspective coherence" is interpreted as the property of producing mutually consistent first-person statements under repeated independent sampling.
- SA2: "Simulated selfhood" denotes the production of self-referential linguistic forms without a self-aware agent as their source.

This explicit structure follows the recommendation of Bunge (1973, p. 67–89) that a scientific theory require interpretive (semantic) postulates to connect its formal symbols to empirical reality — a requirement ignored by purely operationalist and purely formalist approaches.

## 7. Connection to the Broader Research Program

This study is part of a broader research program on **artificial pseudo-consciousness and anthropomorphic drift in LLMs** (see also: Prestes, *Deriva Antropomórfica*, 2026; Prestes, *Agência Simulada e Pseudo-Consciência*, 2025). The Bungean framework provides the epistemological spine for the entire program:

| Research question | Bungean category | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Do LLMs have introspective coherence? | Specific testable model | Tested here; evidence negative |
| Does apparent LLM agency constitute genuine intentionality? | Generic theory (philosophy of mind) | Addressed in companion articles |
| What are the regulatory risks of anthropomorphic attribution? | Applied metaphysics + normative analysis | Addressed in legal/governance papers |
